Magic is divided into three types, each with its own system of governing rules. 

First is divine magic, which stems from beyond [[the River]]. Divine magic is sourced from any creature - or possibly principle - there and can be Good, Evil, Lawful, or Chaotic in nature. Clerics, most heralds, and some warlocks utilize divine magic.

Second is arcane magic, which recognizes the rules that govern the inner planes and manipulates them for the caster's benefit. Arcane magic is a neutral tool; it can be used for different purposes, but does not serve a cosmic one. Wizards, sorcerers, and some warlocks use arcane magic. 

Third is intrinsic magic, which stems from the caster or another creature. Intrinsic magic serves the caster and is aligned accordingly. Bards, druids, and the occasional heralds rely on intrinsic magic. 

> **Special cases!**
> Warlocks' form of magic is determined by their patron. Sorcerers' powers emerge from their bloodline, but rely on arcane rules of casting. Druids pull intrinsic magic from themselves and the natural environment that surrounds them. Heralds typically have committed themselves to a principle and pull from it to power their magic, which would make their magic divine; however, exceptions have been identified. Witches blend the three strands of magic, creating unique, flexible results. 

Use of magic is uncommon among ordinary folk. The occasional person has learned a cantrip in the course of their daily life, and certain heritages tend toward more magic in everyday life than others. Most common is intrinsic magic - a seamster who takes advantage of *mending* or a piper who has learned *circular breathing*. Then comes arcane magic - a scribe who learned *calculate*, for example, would find themselves in high demand. Divine magic is rarest, but those of great faith are sometimes gifted with some magic. 
### Psionics
Psionics are not magic; rather, they speak to the ability of a creature to bring [[the River]] into the Material Plane. If one in a ten has a magical gift, and only one in a hundred of those ever achieves mastery beyond cantrips, humanoids with psionic gifts are even rarer. Species of monsters have more consistent psionic abilities; aboleths, [[Doppelganger|Doppelgangers]], and mind flayers in particular have innate psionics which allow them to wreak havoc upon the Material Plane.

Troublingly, these innate psionics sometimes cause profound personality shifts. In rare cases, a psionically gifted creature will become noticeably more humanoid-like in personality, potentially forswearing its brethren. It is theorized that this is a result of prolonged psionic exposure to humanoids while on the Material Plane; it is further theorized that such exposure can prompt the development of a soul where there was none before.
