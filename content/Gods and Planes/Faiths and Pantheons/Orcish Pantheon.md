Orcs think of the world as a creation built upon the four elements. Their pantheon is eleven: the Great Mother Luthic, her two husbands, and her children by both of them. One of her husbands, Ilneval, is the father of four children, collectively called the Orsinium. The other, Gruumsh, is the father of the other four, collectively called the House of Troubles. Each child by each husband represents one of the four elements.

Orcs believe that the elements are unwilling participants in creation, and orcish druids in particular see their work as continuing that of the gods. Orcs of any persuasion who accomplish great deeds are venerated as paragons of orcdom and, according to legend, live on after death as part of whichever god they claim. Which orcs precisely have been paragons is the source of vicious debate amongst orcish scholars. 

The Orcish gods are as follows:

Luthic: the Great Mother, goddess of chieftans and queens. She watches over life and death and is invoked at birth and during funerary rites.
Ilneval: the Warlord, the protector who teaches honor, strategy, and cunning.
Gruumsh: The Watcher, who teaches retribution, conquest, and emnity. 

Orsinium, fathered by Ilneval:
Kansif: Air. The goddess of hunt and survival. Beloved by rangers.
Neega: Fire. The goddess of joy, love, passion, and excellence. Sometimes seen as a muse.
Vola: Water. The goddess of music, poetry, and history. Beloved by bards and scholars.
Ruhk: Earth. The god of strength to endure hardship.

House of Troubles, fathered by Gruumsh:
Bahgtru: Earth. God of raw strength unfettered by knowledge.
Yutrus: Water. God of disease and pestilence. 
Shagrus: Air. God of deception and lies. 
Shautha: Fire. Goddess of hatred and berserker rage.
