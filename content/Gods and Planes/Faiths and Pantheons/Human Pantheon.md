Humans worship a seven-part pantheon of gods that embody cosmic roles: the Caretaker, the Warrior, the Trickster, the Wild Steward, the Wisdom-keeper, the Stranger, and the Child.  Many will keep a shrine to all seven and leave them offerings of candles, fruits, sweets, or coin.

The Caretaker handles the domain of the home and the family, and is often portrayed as a woman kneeling by a hearth. In most houses, she is invoked over meals, during cooking, and before bed. In Samarach, the Caretaker is given the name of Celesile (CEH-le-sil).

The Warrior handles the domain of conflict and is portrayed as both man and woman, with people usually invoking them based on their own gender to invite strength. They are invoked before or during battle, or at the funeral of one who died in combat. In Samarach, the Warrior is given the name of Phethesi (fe-THES-ee).

The Trickster handles chaos, randomness, and games of chance. They are portrayed as genderless (or, sometimes, as intersex, not always with the kindest of intentions) and invoked when gambling or taking a risk. In Samarach, the Trickster is named Ngozi (n-GOZ-i)

The Wild Steward guards the wilderness and is a favorite of foragers, rangers, and others who spend their time in the wilds. He is usually portrayed as an old man with a walking stick and is invoked to protect wild spaces or ensure a plentiful hunt. In Samarach, the Wild Steward is called Tsebo (SEH-bo).

The Wisdom-keeper handles the domain of knowledge and is often portrayed as a wise old woman with a heavy robe. She is invoked before study, and most certainly before taking a test. In Samarach, she is known as Saba.

The Stranger is rarely invoked, but usually is portrayed as an enigmatic man of varying ages. The Stranger handles the domain of the unknown and as such is invoked only for strength when facing down great fear. It is not uncommon, in a household pantheon, for the Stranger to be set away from the others, but given gifts as a form of assuagement. In Samarach, the Stranger is called Zeray.

Finally, the Child is portrayed as any age from infant to young child and handles the domain of innocence. The Child may be asked to preserve a peace or oversee emotional healing or division. In Samarach, the Child is called Mawael, but in [[Mzali]], he is believed to be incarnate in Walkena (and male, rather than genderless). There, calling the Child by any other name is punishable by death.
