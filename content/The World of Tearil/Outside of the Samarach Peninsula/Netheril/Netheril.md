Netheril is a magocracy of humans founded on the simple belief that magic is power and those with power rule. This has led to a profound stratification of classes, where sorcerers hold the highest rank, followed by wizards; all other forms of magic are considered to be subservient to arcane magic. Netherese warlocks have often come into their power as a way to guarantee social mobility. 

At Netheril's founding, mages were servant-kings, elevated to their position because of their abilities and swearing solemn oaths to their people. As time has passed, however, this has changed. The cultivation of magical power has resulted in ever more of a gulf between those who have it - or who have the money and existing power to obtain tutelage for their children - and those who do not. 

A hundred years ago, the first floating cities took to the skies, and the mages left behind their people for good. The Mundane, as those without magic are called, thought that they would perhaps get a reprieve, but taxes continued to rise and their magocratic overlords began to raise the dead to labor to produce the materials necessary to fuel the floating cities. Now, whole generations may labor in the fields alongside one another, living alongside dead. Still, the hunger for rarer and more luxurious resources has caused Netheril to annex its smaller neighbors as vassal-states.  [[Thar]], [[Sembia]], and most recently the twin kingdoms of [[Cormanthar]] and the [[Dalelands]] have pledged themselves to Netheril, fearing the fire that could rain down from the sky if they did not acquiesce. 

Netheril is led by the Glorious Council underneath an Emperor. Each city's leader has a seat, with the most powerful sorcerer elevated to the rank of Emperor. If another member of the Council wishes to challenge the Emperor for their title, they must consult the one Nether Scroll still displayed openly to the public. The one who is able to create a grander wonder from the scroll's power is the Emperor. However, even the strongest mages struggle to use the power of the scroll, and an inexperienced challenger may find their eyeballs burned out of their skull before they can even attempt to conjure. 

Because of the power of Netherese mages, religion has fallen out of favor amongst the elite. The most arrogant believe that they are themselves only inches away from godhood, or at least from being able to challenge a god. Among the common folk, however, religion is a powerful comfort.
