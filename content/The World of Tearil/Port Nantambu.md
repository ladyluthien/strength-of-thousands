Port Nantambu is the bustling coastal city that hosts the Magaambya. While the school is the heart of the city, a bustling commercial hub exists outside of it, governed by a council of Seven (a number originally chosen for the seven human gods). Attitudes towards the Magaambya vary - most people are proud of the school, but a substantial minority find them arrogant or dangerous. Because of the strong trade in magical creatures, substances, and items, Port Nantambu is a great place to study the weird and wonderful, although outside of the Magaambya, apprenticeships are more common. 

Outside of the school, Port Nantambu enjoys a vibrant musical and performance culture. ~~Your DC-based GM says it's gogo ok~~. It's common for colorful flags and glass chimes to adorn buildings, and small stages offer opportunities for experimental theatre performances. Disagreements are handled by the Chime-Ringers, the closest thing to a police force that the city has; for really dangerous situations, the Tempest-Sun mages lend their expertise, but everyone prefers that such intervention be limited. Tales of magocracies - including in [[Mzali]] - tend to restrain excessive intervention. 

While Port Nantambu has no walls and only the lightest of defenses, few have ever attacked it. A concentration of powerful magic-users will do that - but beyond that, a scrying tower on the edges of the Magaambya houses the Watcher's Stone, set in place by Old-Mage Jatembe when he founded the school. How it works is not publicized, even to students, but it seems that the Magaambya is aware of any threat to the city almost as soon as it is conceived, and a concentration of *prepared* powerful magic-users is suicide to confront. 

Highlights in the city include: 

### The Middle Road
The central corridor for the city - that is, besides the canals and aqueducts - the middle road runs through every central neighborhood and on an average day is jam-packed with people. Traders, panhandlers, tourists, and scam artists compete with pop-up vendors selling food, trinkets, jewelry, and "magical" items. The exception is every few weeks, when the street clears for races; sometimes on foot, but more often on the back of dinosaurs or griffons. Then, the side streets and edges are packed, but the center is kept clear - ending up underfoot at a race is a fast way to gruesome injury or death. 
### The Civic League Boathouse
Equally popular are the canal races, but those tend to cover shorter distances (and, naturally, occur in the canals). These races are hosted by the Civic League Boathouse, an open-membership dockhall in one of the neighboring lakes. Anyone can pay a small fee to reserve a berth, and it is here that the small, colorful canoes that navigate Nantambu's waterways are built and maintained. Taking a canoe into an aqueduct is technically a crime, but the fine can only be leveled if the canoeist is caught, and as a result on fine days it is common for youths to race each other through the elevated aqueducts, whooping the whole way. 
### The Carnivorous Gardens
Initially a side project of a Magaambya student, the Carnivorous Gardens are precisely what they sound like. Popular amongst tourists and researchers alike, the Carnivorous Gardens house the widest variety of plants in Samarach, and possibly in Tearil. 

### The Black Crowned Crane
In an expensive suburb of Port Nantambu, the Black Crowned Crane is the place to see and be seen. Performing there is the dream of many a Nantambuan bard or dancer.  On the weekends, the top theatre troupes perform plays. Because of the glamor surrounding the establishment, there are always rumors about its proprietor. 

### The Dance Courts
These open areas are set aside for anyone to use, and most of the musicians and dancers get their start here. Any musician or group can pull up with their instrument(s) and play; if their music appeals, dancers - or really, anyone who wants to move - congregate. On fine days, the courts are a whirlwind of sound and movement, with musicians collaborating on-the-fly to create the distinctive sound of Nantambu. 

### The Last Chance Shop
On the outskirts of Port Nantambu, the Last Chance Shop preys on unprepared or inexpert jungle explorers. Rumors swirl that on top of scamming its customers, it is a front for smuggling, but they have yet to be substantiated.
