The Magaambya observes year-round classes; eleven weeks of classes (still called a semester), followed by a week of vacation and feasting to honor the new season. Attending year-round is actually fairly rare; many students take one of the four semesters off to engage in independent research, visit home, or simply decompress from their studies. Studying full-time is reserved for the most dedicated and academically inclined of students.

New students join at the beginning of a semester for a trial week, during which they are called **initiates** and do not take classes. Instead, they complete an introductory task for each of the five Academic Branches. At the end of this first week, they are formally welcomed into the Magaambya and become Attendants, selecting their branch at that time. Attendants must take twelve classes, typically spread across four semesters, to graduate to Conversants, and another eight, also typically spread across four semesters, to graduate to Lore-Keepers.

**Attendants** are traditional students who spend most of their time attending classes. However, Magaambya classes are hands-on, practical things, and many teachers take their students on field trips of a week or more - to the occasional displeasure of other teachers, as coordinating academic calendars is made difficult by this hands-on approach. Students can attend any classes they choose, regardless of their Academic Branch. However, a student who finds themselves repeatedly attending classes of a different Academic Branch and neglecting their own may find themselves being called in for a kind conversation about where their passion truly lies.

At the end of the semester, Attendants demonstrate their aptitude on exams through a series of four skill checks depending on the branch. This aptitude is determined, in part, by how well they performed during the semester. Each week, a student has three options: Slack Off, Study, or Take a Chance.

* Slacking Off allows a student to adventure or pursue a different downtime activity, but does not contribute to a final exam score.
* Studying grants a +1 to an exam skill check of their choice, which can be added after the roll is made.
* A student who wishes to both adventure and perform well academically can Take A Chance, making a single skill check of the Narrator's choosing (depending on the class, branch, and instructor) that represents writing an essay by the seat of their pants, submitting a novel mathematical proof, or going off-book in the creation of a spell. On a success, the student gains a +1 as if they had studied; on a failure, they take an -1 - and may incur their instructor's ire. Using anything to influence these rolls is considered academic dishonesty.

Any student who takes more than three classes makes the exam rolls for one of them with disadvantage. Successfully passing a class grants a skill specialty in that class.

**Conversants** still attend classes, but these are smaller and more research-intensive. A student may take an additional Downtime activity at half progression without a penalty, and any student who takes more than two classes makes the exam rolls for them with disadvantage. Additionally, adventuring is no longer considered as Slacking Off; conversants are expected to do things outside the Magaambya, and the time to do it is reflected by the smaller courseload. Field trips become more common. Additionally, teachers for Conversants are more likely to be Lore-Keepers, rather than full professors; highly specialized experts with less pedagogical training, but more subject-matter expertise.

**Lore-Keepers** are technically graduates of the Magaambya, and for some, this is where their time ends as they return to their home communities to take on a leadership role there. However, those who wish to increase their Academic Prestige, continue to take advantage of the Magaambya's resources, or are simply exceptional are often asked to teach Conversant classes and guest-lecture at Initiate classes. Lore-Keepers represent the Magaambya, often far outside of its walls, and full professors are recruited from their ranks. (For your purposes, you are likely to be Lore-Keepers by 8th level or so. This represents a significant accomplishment; however, there is still much to be learned.)
